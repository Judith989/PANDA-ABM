See how drivers search, park and queue before you change a street or a lot.

PANDA is an agent-based parking simulator. Every driver has their own preferences, constraints and decision rule. They search, compete for spaces, walk to their destination and leave, in a 3D model of real Laramie sites.

No sign-in. Simulations run in your browser; nothing you enter is uploaded.

Watch decisions unfold

Start a live simulation and follow any driver in 3D: their route, the spaces they saw, why they slowed down and where they walked.

Test planning ideas

Change time limits, parking demand, how long people stay, initially parked vehicles, space closures and through traffic.

Find the queues

Turn on traffic interactions to see following gaps, junction yielding, signals, pedestrian crossings, entry waits and sustained jams by street block.

Compare fairly

Matched comparisons reuse the same drivers and initial occupancy across information services or planning scenarios, with bootstrap intervals.

Quick start

  1. Choose an environment at the top of the left panel. The default is the Walmart lot corrected from satellite imagery; the original estimate used in the published study, a fictional transfer layout and downtown Laramie streets are also available.
  2. Set the scenario: decision model, parking information, occupancy, drivers and arrival gap. Planning options sit further down the panel.
  3. Start a live simulation to watch it unfold, or compute a saved replay to scrub back and forth.
  4. Click a vehicle or space on the map to inspect it. Pick a driver below the map to see their full route and walking path.
  5. Open Matched comparisons to compare information services or planning scenarios across replications, then download JSON or CSV.

Read the results with care

  • Driver behaviour follows published parking-choice models but is not calibrated to observed Laramie drivers.
  • Layouts are estimated from imagery and OpenStreetMap, not surveyed. Downtown off-street inventory is incomplete.
  • Traffic, signal timing and through-traffic rates are assumptions. Use jam results to compare scenarios, not as forecasts.
  • The 3D view shows simulated conditions, not live occupancy.

Lines on the map are the modelled driving network. A jam means at least two vehicles held below 0.5 m/s by traffic on the same aisle for at least 5 seconds. Violet: a jam is happening there now, drawn where the queued vehicles are. Lavender: that aisle had a jam earlier in this run. Yellow: no jam, but vehicles there have lost at least 30 seconds in total to traffic. Thin light grey: free-flowing. Bay entry spurs are never coloured. The model is uncalibrated, so compare scenarios rather than treating colours as forecasts.

A jam requires at least two traffic-constrained vehicles below 0.5 m/s in an aisle for at least 5 simulated seconds. Single-vehicle delays are recorded separately. Nearby vehicles use geometric visibility; buildings and mirrors are not modeled. This is not a calibrated traffic forecast.
